FAMILY 01 — THE ENGINE

Workflows that create algorithms.

These are the systems that literally produce new algorithms. Every one runs the same loop: propose variants of a procedure → evaluate against a signal the procedure can't game → keep only what measurably wins → repeat on unseen instances. That loop is GOLD at machine speed.

AlphaEvolve

Evolutionary LLM agent proposes and evaluates code variants; discovered improved matrix-mult and attention kernels.

arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

An evolutionary agent that uses a language model to propose code variants and an evaluator to score them, keeping only variants that measurably improve. It runs the propose–evaluate–keep loop that GOLD formalises. Its reported results include improved matrix-multiplication and attention kernels.

How it maps to GOLD

CREATES GOLD — the loop itself is GOLD at machine speed. The win still needs GOLD's receipt: a frozen holdout and a paired win, so the kernel gain is proved real, not benchmark gaming.

How to use it

When you evolve code, freeze your evaluator and holdout before the run, then keep only variants that win on the frozen set.

FAMILY 02 — THE RECEIPT

Verification integrity: how you know it's real.

The statistics and testing science behind the gates. Campbell's law, the winner's curse, and forking paths tell you why single runs lie; always-valid inference, holdouts, k-fold, and interleaving are the mathematics of proof; mutation, metamorphic, fuzzing, chaos, and canary are the proof for code and systems.

GATE 05↗

Campbell's law

The more an outcome measure is used for decision-making, the more it is corrupted by gaming and pressure.

wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell's_law
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

When an outcome measure is used for decision-making, it gets corrupted by gaming and pressure. People optimise the measure instead of the outcome it stood for. The more the measure matters, the more it is gamed.

How it maps to GOLD

Goodhart gate (GATE 05). GOLD demands proof the win is not the metric being gamed: guardrail metrics, a sealed truth, and a paired win that survives.

How to use it

Before adopting a metric as a target, name one way it could be gamed, and add a guardrail metric that must not degrade.

GATE 04↗

Winner's curse

The selected "best" tends to be the luckiest outlier, overstating the true effect.

wikipedia.org/wiki/Winner's_curse
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

When you select the "best" from many candidates, the winner tends to be the luckiest outlier, not the truly best. The selection itself inflates the apparent effect. The winner's measured advantage overstates its true advantage.

How it maps to GOLD

Paired win gate (GATE 04). GOLD demands the paired win survive bootstrap and correction, so luck is averaged out.

How to use it

When you pick a winner from many candidates, re-test the winner on fresh data before claiming the win.

GATE 01↗

Garden of forking paths

Hidden researcher degrees of freedom exploited until p<0.05 appears.

wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_of_forking_paths
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

Hidden researcher degrees of freedom — choices of model, metric, cutoff, exclusion — are exploited, consciously or not, until a p-value below 0.05 appears. The path taken is chosen after seeing the data. The result looks significant but is selected.

How it maps to GOLD

Commit gate (GATE 01). GOLD demands freeze before you look: lock hypothesis, model, and analysis before seeing outcomes.

How to use it

Before you look at outcomes, write down your hypothesis, model, and analysis plan, and do not change them after seeing the data.

What it is

A method that gives confidence sequences valid at every stopping time, so false positives stay controlled even if you peek and stop early. It removes the penalty for looking. It lets you monitor continuously without inflating error.

How to use it

When you monitor an experiment continuously, use always-valid confidence sequences, not fixed-sample tests checked early.

GATE 06↗

arxiv.org/abs/1808.00114

What it is

Metrics that are not the objective but must not degrade while you optimise the objective. They guard against wins that break something else. They are the cost gate's cousin: they keep a win honest.

Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

A test that runs two identical variants to calibrate variance and false-positive rate before running real experiments. If identical variants "differ," your measurement is broken. It is a smoke test for the experiment system.

SEEDS+1↗

Holdout methodology

Evaluate once on a frozen holdout; never iterate on the test set.

wikipedia.org/wiki/Holdout_method
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

Evaluating once on a frozen holdout set, and never iterating on the test set. The holdout is touched once, for the final verdict. Any iteration on it turns it into training data.

How it maps to GOLD

SEEDS+1 protocol — 5 fresh seeds plus one frozen holdout run. GOLD demands the final verdict come from data never used for tuning.

How to use it

Freeze a holdout before tuning, and evaluate on it exactly once, at the end.

kaggle.com/docs/competitions
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

The public leaderboard is a slice of the test set; optimising it overfits that slice. Only the private leaderboard measures the truth. Public-score chasing is holdout gaming.

How it maps to GOLD

SEEDS+1 protocol. GOLD demands the verdict come from a frozen set never used for tuning.

How to use it

Treat any public leaderboard as training feedback, and judge only by a private, frozen set.

GOODHART↗

Novelty effect

Users react to "new" regardless of quality, inflating short-term metrics that decay.

wikipedia.org/wiki/Novelty_effect
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

Users react to "new" regardless of quality, inflating short-term metrics that then decay. A new feature wins at first because it is new, not because it is better. The lift fades as novelty wears off.

How it maps to GOLD

Goodhart gate. GOLD demands proof the win is not novelty: measure past the novelty window.

How to use it

When you ship something new, wait out the novelty window before measuring, and report the settled number, not the launch spike.

GOODHART↗

wikipedia.org/wiki/Primacy_effect
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

Early items strongly shape evaluation, distorting order-sensitive metrics. What is seen first is judged more favourably, regardless of quality. Order becomes a confounder.

How it maps to GOLD

Goodhart gate. GOLD demands the win not be an order artefact: randomise order, or account for it.

How to use it

When order could matter, randomise presentation order, and check that the win survives across orders.

What it is

Loss aversion

Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good.

wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_theory
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good. People work harder to avoid a loss than to gain the same amount. It is the asymmetry behind prospect theory.

How it maps to GOLD

DEALER. GOLD frames the loss honestly — the cost of shipping an unverified claim is real.

How to use it

When you present a standard, name the real loss of skipping it (a claim that fails in production), not an invented one.

Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

The first number seen disproportionately biases all later value judgments. The anchor sets the frame, even when it is arbitrary. Later numbers are judged against it.

How it maps to GOLD

DEALER. GOLD anchors on the real number — the measured receipt, not a cherry-picked one.

How to use it

Anchor on the real measured number (the holdout result, the paired win), and show how it was measured.

DEALER↗

Sunk cost fallacy

People keep investing in something they've already put effort into.

wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunk_cost
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

People keep investing in something they have already put effort into, even when stopping would be better. Past effort is treated as a reason to continue. It is a fallacy because past cost is gone either way.

How it maps to GOLD

DEALER. GOLD names sunk cost as a trap to avoid — a failing approach does not become right because you spent time on it.

How to use it

When an approach fails its gates, stop it, however much you spent on it. The receipt decides, not the sunk cost.

DEALER↗

IKEA effect

People overvalue things they partially created themselves.

wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEA_effect
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

People overvalue things they partially created themselves. Building part of it makes it feel more valuable. The effort becomes part of the value.

How it maps to GOLD

DEALER. GOLD uses the IKEA effect honestly — visitors who run the gates themselves value the receipt more, because they did the work.

How to use it

Let visitors run the gates themselves (check the receipt, verify the holdout), rather than asking them to take your word for it.

DEALER↗

Endowment effect

People ascribe higher value to things merely because they own them.

wikipedia.org/wiki/Endowment_effect
Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

People ascribe higher value to things merely because they own them. Ownership alone inflates perceived value. It is the counterpart to loss aversion.

How it maps to GOLD

DEALER. GOLD refuses to exploit endowment — it does not make visitors feel they own a result they have not verified.

How to use it

Do NOT make visitors feel they own a result before they have verified it. The receipt is earned by verification, not by possession.

Peak-end rule

Detail: what it is, GOLD mapping, how to use it

What it is

Experiences are judged by their most intense moment and their ending, not by the average. The peak and the end dominate memory. The rest fades.

How it maps to GOLD

DEALER. GOLD designs the peak (the verified win) and the end (the receipt) to be real.

How to use it

Make the peak a real verified win and the ending a real receipt, so the memory is of something true.

The synthesis — the algorithm that makes gold

Family 1 is the engine. Propose → evaluate against an ungameable signal → keep winners → repeat. AlphaEvolve, FunSearch, and the Darwin Gödel Machine are GOLD at machine speed — but none of them ship the verification discipline as a standalone standard. That gap is real, and it's the positioning.

Family 2 is the receipt. freeze before you look → same fight for both sides → score against a sealed truth → demand the paired win survives bootstrap and correction → prove it isn't the prior, the proxy, or the compute → repeat on seeds never seen.

Family 3 is the dealer. Hook with the gap, anchor on the real number, keep the loop open — and let the honesty be the hook. Family 1 is mostly DeepMind/Sakana work; GOLD is the evaluation-and-verification discipline every one of those loops needs but none of them ship.
